A Note on the Pieces

Eight figures stand on each side of the board. Seven of them fight; one of them is fought for. The seven combat pieces — the Warrior, the Sage, the Traitor, the Fool, the Guardian, the Dreamer, the Soldier — move in patterns drawn from the long history of chess. The eighth piece, the Lover, breaks that tradition entirely. She walks only toward the Heart at the centre of the board. She cannot be captured by any enemy. She is the only piece that can win the game.

This codex describes each of the eight in turn. You will find, for every piece, a rendering of its sculpted form, a diagram of its movement, a profile of its combat reach, and a short note on its role in the formation we call The Embrace.

Read it slowly. The pieces are simple, but their interactions are not.

The Lover

Sacred · uncapturable · the crown-piece.
I · VIII
Sculpture

Movement

1 cell · only toward the Heart.

She walks with her eyes lowered. She does not look back. She moves a single step at a time, always closer to the centre of the board, and a force she does not name pulls her there. No army can stop her, no piece can strike her down. Her path may be blocked, her companions slain, her arrival delayed — but she cannot be removed. So the contest, in the end, is never whether she will reach the Heart. It is whether her friends will arrive in time to stand beside her.

Combat Profile

Range
1 cell
Direction
Strictly toward the Heart (decreasing distance)
Captures
By displacement, on any enemy in her path
Captured?
Never. No enemy may move onto her cell.
Win-piece?
Yes — the only one.

Role in the Embrace

She is the centrepiece. Without her at the Heart, the Embrace cannot form. Bring her to (d, 4) as soon as you can with two of your other pieces ringed around her — that is the win.

"What walks alone is what cannot be touched."

The Warrior

Steady and close at hand · the king's reach.
II · VIII
Sculpture

Movement

1 cell · any of the 8 directions.

A short man with a long blade. He stands close to whoever he is told to guard, and he moves one square at a time in any direction, never further. He is the chess king's reach without the chess king's fragility — a soldier whose strength is in patience, not in pace. The Warrior's best work is done in the Lotus, where he stands beside the Lover and any enemy who would dislodge her must come to him first.

Combat Profile

Range
1 cell
Direction
Any of 8 (orthogonal + diagonal)
Captures
By landing on any enemy
Chess analogue
King (without the check restriction)
Best in
The Lotus, as a Lover's escort

Role in the Embrace

A perfect escort. He moves slowly, but the slow piece in the Lotus is hardest to dislodge — every cell he stands on is a cell an enemy must spend a full turn to clear. Bring him to the Heart's neighbourhood by mid-game.

"He stands where he is set. That is the whole of his courage."

The Sage

Sees along the diagonals · the bishop's gaze.
III · VIII
Sculpture

Movement

Any number of cells · along the 4 diagonals.

He was born on a coloured square and he will die on one of the same shade. The Sage moves only along diagonals — long, clean lines that cut across the board — and so he sees half of the board his whole life and never the other half. A Sage on a light cell threatens the diagonals into the Lotus from one angle; a Sage on a dark cell threatens the opposite angle. Where you place him in setup decides which half of the war he will fight.

Combat Profile

Range
Unlimited · stops at first piece
Direction
4 diagonals
Captures
First enemy in the path
Chess analogue
Bishop
Limitation
Bound to one cell colour forever

Role in the Embrace

From the right diagonal, the Sage can reach the Lotus in one move from far away. Use him for surprise arrivals — but remember he can only land on cells of his own colour, which means only 4 of the 8 Lotus cells are open to any single Sage.

"He follows the long line. The line follows him back."

The Traitor

Stalks the straight lines · the rook's reach.
IV · VIII
Sculpture

Movement

Any number of cells · along rows and columns.

The Traitor moves in straight lines. He takes a row, or he takes a column, or he takes one and then the other on a later turn. He is named for what he does to a fellow soldier on his own column — he passes through, with intent. In the open board he is the longest-range threat, and on the d-column (the road to the Heart) he is the most feared piece of all, because he can reach the Lotus directly from his starting row.

Combat Profile

Range
Unlimited · stops at first piece
Direction
4 orthogonals (rows + columns)
Captures
First enemy in the path
Chess analogue
Rook
Best file
The d-column — direct road to the Heart

Role in the Embrace

If your setup places him on the d-column, he commands the path to the Heart from move 1. Few enemies can afford to leave him alive in such a position — so he will be hunted, and he will hunt back.

"He walks the line you laid for someone else."

The Fool

Jumps where no one expects · the knight's leap.
V · VIII
Sculpture

Movement

L-shape jumps (2 + 1) · jumps over anything.

He wears bells. He laughs at walls. He is the only piece in PYAAR who can leap over another, and so he is the only piece you cannot fence in. The Fool is wonderful at slipping into the Lotus when the centre is jammed with pieces — he flies past them all and lands in the cell beside the Lover. Lose him early and you lose your best surprise. Use him to deliver the second escort at the very end.

Combat Profile

Range
L-shape (2 + 1) jumps
Direction
8 possible L-jumps
Captures
By landing
Chess analogue
Knight
Unique trait
Jumps over any pieces in between

Role in the Embrace

The cleanest path to the Lotus when the board is dense. He arrives where no other piece could. Save him for the final escort delivery when the centre is locked.

"The walls were always advice, not law."

The Guardian

A short fierce queen · the temple door-keeper.
VI · VIII
Sculpture

Movement

Up to 2 cells · in any of 8 directions.

Aqueen with a leash. The Guardian moves as a chess queen does — straight lines, diagonals, eight directions — but only as far as two cells, and never further. He carries a heavy mace on his shoulder and he uses it. Within his reach, he is the most dangerous piece on the board; outside it, he is patient. Place him near the Heart in setup and he begins the game as a Lotus threat.

Combat Profile

Range
Up to 2 cells
Direction
Any of 8 (orthogonal + diagonal)
Captures
By landing
Chess analogue
Short queen (range 2)
Best in
Mid-board, clearing Lotus access

Role in the Embrace

The piece that clears the Lotus. He can capture an enemy escort and land in their cell — converting denial into your own formation in a single move.

"The door he keeps is the centre. Nothing else."

The Dreamer

Quick · harmless · the moonlit scout.
VII · VIII
Sculpture

Movement

Up to 3 cells · any direction · no capture.

She walks in dreams. She moves three full cells in any straight line — fast, gliding — but she cannot strike a single blow. She is a pacifist, a scout, a flag-runner. Her gift is reach without violence. In the Embrace she is the perfect first escort: she crosses the board in two moves and stands in the Lotus while your fighters are still developing.

Combat Profile

Range
Up to 3 cells
Direction
Any of 8 (orthogonal + diagonal)
Captures
None. Must end on empty cell.
Chess analogue
No direct one — a long-range pacifist
Best as
First escort to the Lotus

Role in the Embrace

Your fastest deliverable escort. From row 1 she can be on the Lotus by move 2, untouched, ready to wait. The fact that she cannot capture isn't a weakness for this role — she only needs to stand there.

"She crosses the field without lifting a blade."

The Soldier

The vanguard · a pawn that pushes the line.
VIII · VIII
Sculpture

Movement

1 cell straight forward · captures diagonally forward.

He marches and he marches and he never marches back. The Soldier moves a single cell forward, into the enemy's half of the board, and the only way he ever harms an enemy is when one stands at the diagonal in front of him — the chess pawn's only weapon. He is slow, irreversible, stubborn. In PYAAR he no longer carries the crown; that honour belongs to the Lover now. But the line he holds is still the line that wins or loses the centre.

Combat Profile

Range
1 cell forward · 1 diagonal capture
Direction
Strictly forward (P1: +r, P2: −r)
Captures
Diagonal-forward only · pawn style
Chess analogue
Pawn
Limitation
Cannot retreat · cannot move sideways

Role in the Embrace

A slow body for the Lotus. Once he reaches the row in front of the Heart he can step in as an escort, and once there he is hard to move — he never goes back. Best brought up in the late game when the Lover is already close.

"The road only goes one way. That is enough."

A Closing

Eight figures, eight movements, eight stories. They share one board and one centre and one goal-piece, and that is all the rules they need. The contest, in the end, is whether the picture you carry in your mind — Lover at the Heart, two friends beside her — can survive the picture your opponent carries.
